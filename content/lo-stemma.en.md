Beneath a vault of lapis lazuli studded with golden stars — the very one Giotto
painted in the **Scrovegni Chapel** — the emblem gathers into a single field the
sky of Padua and the board of Go. The stars, set in a regular weave, are nothing
but the **intersections of the goban**: the playing surface becomes a firmament,
and every move a light kindled in the dark. The stones you see laid down are a
real game, mounted on the crossings of the vault.

## The sun and the circle

At the centre, within a sunburst, the word **Circolo**: the sun of a community
that gathers and radiates. Around it, arranged like the stars of a cross, the name
— *Goistico*, *Padovano* — and the two protectors who watch from above.

## Master and disciple

They are two men of Padua: **Saint Anthony**, patron of the city, and his
disciple, the **Blessed Luca Belludi**. They face one another across the sun, like
two players on either side of the board. It is the bond at the heart of every art
that is handed down: the one who teaches and the one who learns.

## Things found again

To Saint Anthony one turns for what has been lost and is hoped to be found again.
Here he raises neither a key nor a coin, but a **Go stone**: the smallest of
objects, easily lost among a thousand, precious at just the right moment. And Go is
itself an art of finding — every stone placed is a thing sought and found, every
position the search for the exact point. The gesture is that of blessing, which in
Giotto raises two fingers: and between forefinger and middle finger rests the
stone. To play becomes a blessed act, and to find the move a small grace.

## Black and white

The master holds the **white** stone — White, who answers, who moves second; the
disciple holds the **black** — Black, who opens the game, who dares the first move,
as the pupil learns by daring. In the two colours the whole game is already
written.

## The lily and the book

To Anthony, the **lily**: purity, and with it the fragrance of good doctrine. To
Luca, the **book**: the *Sermones* he helped to compose, and his patronage of
students. For the Circolo is above all a place of study, where one grows by
playing.

## Gold and the cord

The ground is **gold**, as in icons and illuminations: not a colour but a light —
the eternity within which the game, like every serious thing, finds its measure.
And the white cord of three knots, Franciscan poverty, recalls that this art asks
for nothing but attention.
